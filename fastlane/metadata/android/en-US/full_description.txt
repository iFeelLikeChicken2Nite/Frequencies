Frequencies is a modern, stylish internet radio player that reignites the nostalgia of turning a real dial. Save your favorite stations, tune between them with simulated static, and see what is playing. Simple, yet powerful. Decline the algorithm, and rediscover radio.

On top of what typical radio apps do, like streaming from URLs and browsing radio station directories, Frequencies gives you much more:

* Create custom stations from local files, YouTube, Twitch streams and iBroadcast libraries
* A tag system for easily queuing the stations you want
* Built-in song recognition (Song ID, using Shazam's service)
* Cover the forced ads on some stations with your own music
* A regularly updated list of recommended stations, hand-picked by me, a human
* Per-station equalizer
* Volume leveling across stations
* A log of the songs you heard, from Song ID
* Change a station's displayed name and artwork
* Easy, modern sharing options
* Automatic skipping of stations that are down
* Live, timed lyrics
* Artist biographies
* Android Auto and Pebble support
* Sync between your devices on your network

Note that for Android Auto, you'll have to turn on "Unknown sources" in Android Auto's developer settings. This is a Google restriction for apps that don't come from the Play Store, and no app can get around it.

<b>Network services</b>
Song ID uses Shazam's service. You can also choose to download separately hosted non-free files that enhance the experience (off by default, and the app never nags you about it). Because the app uses services like Shazam, YouTube and Twitch, F-Droid marks it NonFreeNet. Everything else works without them: playing, saving, sharing, static, the schedule and Android Auto. Lyrics come from LRCLIB and lyrics.ovh, artist facts from MusicBrainz and Wikipedia, and station search from Radio Browser.
